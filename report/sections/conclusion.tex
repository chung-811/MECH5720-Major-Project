\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built a coded illumination rig that turns an ordinary camera and monitor into a flutter shutter
camera, and a pipeline that reverses the blur and measures the speed of a spinning fan from one
photograph. On the rig, coded light deblurred \rigGapMin~to \rigGapMax~dB (global shutter) and
\rigRsGapMin~to \rigRsGapMax~dB (rolling) better than steady light; at \SI{8}{rpm} a character reader
read \rigReadCodedEight\% of the letters after it against \rigReadSteadyEight\% after steady light; and
the blur gave the speed within 5\% on \rigSpeedWithinCoded~of \rigSpeedAllCoded~and \rigSpeedWithinRsCoded~of
\rigSpeedAllRsCoded~coded captures. On the rig, per row and per frame agreed to within \SI{0.17}{dB} even with the exposure equal to the code, because our camera reads out in less than one bit; in simulation, with a readout of several bits, per frame lost \rowsShortDropA~dB on average while per row came within \rowsMaxMeanGapA~dB of the global shutter. 

With two more months we would replace the monitor with an LED, whose microsecond switching would allow bits short enough to test per row on the rig and codes hundreds of bits long; design codes for the rolling shutter, so that its row timing adds information instead of being corrected for; measure the motor's speed independently; and move from a fan to everyday moving scenes, where the motion must be estimated for every object.
